### "Why you over a bigger automation agency?"

## Pricing Deep Dive

### Tactic 1: Reframe as Investment (ROI Math)
"Let's look at this differently. The project is [price]. Based on what you've told me, you're likely losing [estimate] a month to [specific pain — missed calls, slow follow-up]. That puts the breakeven around [X weeks/months]. Everything after that is pure upside. Want to run the actual numbers together using your specifics?"

### Tactic 2: Cost of Inaction
"Flip the question: what does it cost you to NOT fix this? Every month this stays manual, you're likely losing [specific cost — a missed job, wasted staff hours]. Over a year, that adds up fast. The real question isn't whether you can afford this — it's whether you can afford to keep losing what you're currently losing."

### Tactic 3: Total Cost of Ownership Comparison
"On the surface, DIY tools or hiring someone look cheaper. But factor in the real cost: [Goldenspire: project fee + optional monitoring, fully documented, no ongoing dependency]. [DIY: subscription + your own setup and maintenance time]. [Hiring: salary + benefits + training + turnover risk, still no 24/7 coverage]. When you add it all up, this usually comes out ahead."

"If the full build doesn't fit right now, let's start with the single highest-impact piece — usually intake and follow-up, since that's where most owners feel the pain first. It's scoped smaller, priced accordingly, and you can expand once you see it working. That's actually the lower-risk way to start as a founding client anyway."

### Tactic 5: Payment Terms Flexibility

## Objection Prevention Tactics

1. **Pre-emptive framing:** Lead every first call with the three things prospects worry about before they ask — no long contract, you own everything, 30-day warranty. Deploy at the very start of the discovery call, before pitching any specific service.
2. **Honesty-loaded credibility (in place of social proof):** Since there's no case-study library yet, lead with the founder's real day-job credibility explicitly and early, rather than letting the "new practice" gap surface as a surprise objection later. Deploy in the first 2 minutes of any call.
3. **Discovery-driven selling:** Ask about their actual missed-call/follow-up situation before pitching any service — this naturally narrows the conversation to only what's relevant, which prevents most feature-comparison objections from ever coming up. Deploy immediately after rapport-building, before any pitch.
4. **Mutual, low-commitment next step:** Always end a call with a small, concrete, low-risk next step (a free walkthrough, a scoped single-workflow pilot) rather than asking for a full commitment upfront — this prevents "I need to think about it" from having room to form. Deploy at the close of every call.
5. **Founding-client framing:** Explicitly name the fact that early clients get more attention and better terms in exchange for being a reference later — this reframes "you're new" from a weakness into a reason to act now. Deploy specifically when the "how do I know this works" objection is likely (regulated industries, risk-averse owners).

## Practice Guide

**Role-play scenarios for the 5 hardest objections:**
1. "How do I know this will work for us? You're new." (Objection 14) — hardest because it can't be answered with a fabricated proof point; practice the honest, confident version until it doesn't sound defensive.
2. "We tried something like this before and it didn't work." (Objection 7) — practice actually listening to what broke before pitching anything.

5. "Just not interested." (Objection 15) — practice the graceful, non-pushy exit; the goal is preserving the relationship for a future trigger event, not winning the call.
