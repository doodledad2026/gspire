Our Approach

The 20-30-50 Approach

Most automation projects fail for the same reason: too much focus on the software, not enough on how people actually work. This is the split that guides every build.

20%

Picking the Right Tool

Drawing on hands-on experience across a wide range of AI models and automation platforms to match the right one to what the job actually needs, rather than the flashiest option.

30%

Building It Right

Clean, tested, reliable automation, engineered to run unattended, with a plain-English record of exactly what it does.

50%

Making It Fit

The majority of the work: shaping the system around how you and your team actually operate, so it gets used instead of abandoned after week one.

Five steps. No disruption to how you run today.

Why The Split Matters

"The model or the platform is never the hard part. The hard part is making a system disappear into how your team already works, so it actually gets used."

After The Build

Two ways to work with us.

Every engagement starts the same way: we build the system, test it against real scenarios, and hand it off fully documented. From there, it's your call how hands-on you want to stay.

Build & Handover

We design, build, and deliver a complete system on accounts you own, with a plain-English record of exactly what it does. Once it's live, it's fully yours. Most engagements end right here, with your team driving it going forward.

Build & Monitor

Some businesses would rather not think about it. As an add-on to any build, we monitor the system, catch issues before they become problems, and update or extend it as your business evolves, for a straightforward monthly fee. You get the system without the stress of maintaining it yourself.

Why It Works This Way

Software is the easy part.

Anyone can wire an AI model to a form. The hard part, and the part that decides whether a system actually gets used six months later, is understanding how your team really works day to day: who needs to see what, what happens when something goes wrong, and what would make someone quietly stop using it.

That's why the "fit" phase gets the most weight. A technically perfect automation that doesn't match how your business actually operates gets abandoned. A slightly simpler one that fits perfectly gets used every single day.
