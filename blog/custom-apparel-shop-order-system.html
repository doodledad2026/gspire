How Custom T-Shirt and Print Shops Stop Losing Orders in Text Messages

Your designs are great. The orders shouldn't depend on scrolling back through a text thread.

Share

Most small custom apparel, embroidery and print shops start the same way: a Facebook page, a "text us to order" line and a lot of goodwill. It works until the orders pick up. Then the details live in five different threads, a team order needs sizes from twelve people and a quote you sent last week never got a reply.

We recently set up a system for a custom apparel shop here in South Central PA that had exactly this problem. Here's what changed and how you can do the same.

The three ways orders get lost

  • Missing details. A text that says "can I get 10 shirts?" leaves you chasing design, colors, sizes, placement and a deadline.
  • No reply in time. When you're printing, you're not answering. Buyers often go with whoever answers first (Verse).
  • Quotes that go quiet. You price the job, send it and move on. Nothing reminds you to check back, so the order quietly goes to someone else.

The fix: two forms and one tracker

The shop added two buttons to their online store: Custom Orders and Bulk & Team Orders. Each opens a short form that asks for everything they used to chase: design, shirt style, colors, print placement, sizes, quantity, deadline and pickup or shipping.

When someone submits a form:

  1. The customer gets a friendly confirmation email within a minute, with a request number.
  2. The shop gets an alert with every answer.
  3. The request lands in one tracker sheet, marked New.
  4. Anything needed within 14 days is flagged RUSH, so tight deadlines get answered first.

Customers who have a logo or artwork just reply to the confirmation email and attach it. No accounts or uploads to figure out.

Follow-ups that happen on their own

When the owner sends a quote, they mark the row "Quote Sent." If the customer hasn't answered by day 3, a reminder email lands at 8 AM. If they still haven't answered by day 7, one more reminder. That's it. No CRM to learn, and nothing falls through the cracks. (More on that in the day 3 and day 7 rule.)

Faster new listings

The same project added ready-made product templates in Square, with sizes, colors, price and fabric details already set, plus a saved AI prompt that writes product descriptions in the shop's own voice. A new design now goes live in under 10 minutes.
