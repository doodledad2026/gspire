Build & Handover vs. Build & Monitor: Which Fits Your Business?

One of the first real decisions after deciding to automate isn't which tool to use. It's how hands-on you want to stay once it's live.

Every engagement starts the same way: a system gets designed, built, and tested against real scenarios from your business, not a generic template. Where it goes from there is a real choice, and it's worth making deliberately instead of defaulting into it.

Build & Handover

This is the simpler of the two. We design, build, and deliver a complete system on accounts you own, fully documented in plain English. Once it's live and working, it's entirely yours: your team runs it, makes changes to it, and owns every account it touches. No ongoing relationship required.

This fits you if: you have someone on your team (even part-time) who can own a piece of software going forward, your process is fairly stable, and you'd rather pay once than carry a recurring cost indefinitely.

Build & Monitor

Some businesses would rather not think about it again. As an add-on to any build, this means we monitor the system, catch issues before they become customer-facing problems, and update or extend it as your business evolves, all for a straightforward monthly fee.

This fits you if: nobody on your team has the bandwidth or interest to own a piece of automation, your business changes frequently enough that a static system would go stale, or the cost of something quietly breaking (a missed lead, a broken integration) is high enough that peace of mind is worth paying for.

How to actually decide

Three questions tend to settle it quickly:

  1. If this broke at 6pm on a Friday, who would notice, and who would fix it? If the honest answer is "nobody, until a customer complains," Build & Monitor is probably worth it.
  2. Is your process likely to change in the next 6-12 months? New services, new team structure, new tools: the more change coming, the more a monitored system earns its keep.
  3. Do you actually want to own this, or do you just want it to work? Both are legitimate answers. The mistake is picking Build & Handover because it sounds cheaper, then not having anyone available when something needs attention six months later.

Neither path locks you in. Support after a handover is available on a month-to-month basis if you change your mind later, and monitoring can be scaled back once a system's been stable for a while. See the full breakdown of how we approach every build for the rest of the process.
