How to Get More Google Reviews for Your Local Business (Without Awkward Asking)

Your happiest customers would leave a review. Most of them just never get asked at the right moment.

Share

When someone in Carlisle, York or Gettysburg searches for a business like yours, they compare star ratings and read the newest reviews before they ever call. If your newest review is from last year, you're losing calls to the business above you on the map, even if your work is better.

What customers look for in 2026

BrightLocal's 2026 Local Consumer Review Survey shows how much reviews now drive the decision (summary by PinMeTo):

  • 85% are more likely to use a business after reading positive reviews.
  • 74% look for reviews from the last three months.
  • 31% will only use businesses rated 4.5 stars or higher.
  • 80% are more likely to use a business that replies to all of its reviews, but 50% are put off by generic, templated replies.

The takeaway: you need a steady stream of recent reviews, and you need to answer them like a person.

Why most businesses don't get reviews

It isn't because customers are unhappy. It's because nobody asks at the right moment. The best time is right after the job is done or the order is picked up, when the customer is happiest. A week later, they've moved on.

A simple system that runs on its own

  1. Trigger: when you mark a job complete or an order picked up, the system sends a short, friendly message.
  2. The ask: one sentence, their first name, and a direct link to your Google review page. One tap, no searching.
  3. A way to reach you: the same message gives every customer the review link and a direct way to contact you if anything wasn't right, so problems get fixed fast.
  4. Reply to every review: keep a few starting points, then personalize each reply with the customer's name and the job.

Don't forget your Google Business Profile

Reviews work best on a profile that's complete: correct hours, service area, services with prices where it makes sense, recent photos and a link to your request form. Many local profiles are missing half of that.

Two rules to follow: never offer discounts or gifts in exchange for reviews, and never send only your happy customers to Google. Google's policies don't allow either. Ask everyone the same way, and fix problems when customers tell you about them.
